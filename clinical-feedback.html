Clinical Feedback/Research

Research & Scientific Rationale

The design of Akoya Medical Eyewear is informed by published research examining patient anxiety, visual exposure to medical procedures, fear responses, vasovagal reactions, and distraction-based interventions.

Visual Exposure and Fear During Procedures

Research has shown that patients who are instructed to look at a needle during a medical procedure report higher levels of fear than patients who look away, even when pain levels are similar.

References

Mithal P, Simmons P, Cornelissen T, et al. To look or not to look during vaccination: A pilot randomized trial. Canadian Journal of Pain. 2018;2(1):1-8.

https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8730672/

Visual Distraction and Procedural Anxiety

Studies have demonstrated that visual distraction techniques can help reduce anxiety and fear during invasive medical procedures by directing a patient's attention away from the procedural field.

References

Kilic A, Brown A, Aras I, et al. Using Virtual Technology for Fear of Medical Procedures: A Systematic Review of the Effectiveness of Virtual Reality-Based Interventions. Annals of Behavioral Medicine. 2021;55(11):1062-1079.

https://doi.org/10.1093/abm/kaab016

Virtual Reality and Procedural Comfort

Multiple studies have reported reductions in procedural anxiety when immersive visual environments are used to limit exposure to medical stimuli.

References

Eijlers R, Utens EMWJ, Staals LM, et al. Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Virtual Reality in Pediatrics: Effects on Pain and Anxiety. Anesthesia & Analgesia. 2019;129(5):1344-1353.

https://doi.org/10.1213/ANE.0000000000004165

Vasovagal Reactions During Medical Procedures

Vasovagal reactions, including dizziness, nausea, and fainting, are recognized complications during certain medical procedures. Published reviews have identified anxiety and procedural stress as important contributing factors.

References

Malave M, Brian B, Vrooman B. Vasovagal Reactions during Interventional Pain Management Procedures—A Review of Pathophysiology, Incidence, Risk Factors, Prevention, and Management. Medical Sciences. 2022;10(3):39.

https://doi.org/10.3390/medsci10030039

Design Philosophy

Akoya Medical Eyewear was developed around a simple concept:

If visual exposure to medical procedures can contribute to fear and anxiety, selectively limiting the patient's view of the procedural field may improve comfort while preserving communication with clinical staff.

The device was designed to block the lower field of vision while maintaining an unobstructed upper field of view, allowing patients to remain visually connected with caregivers and their surroundings.

Important Notice

Akoya Medical Eyewear is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease or medical condition. The studies referenced above evaluate visual exposure, distraction techniques, procedural anxiety, and vasovagal responses generally. Additional clinical research may be required to evaluate the specific effects of Akoya Medical Eyewear in individual medical settings.
